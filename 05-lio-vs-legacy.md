## The core argument in one line

Legacy procurement technology — every generation of it — **accelerated human work**. Lio's generation **executes the work itself**. The bottleneck was never the system of record; it was manual human execution *between* the systems ("Enterprise Systems Aren't the Bottleneck — Manual Execution Is").

## The "digitization illusion"

Lio's sharpest critique of 20 years of procurement tech: interfaces changed (paper → portal → cloud) but the **operating model never did**. A human still interprets the request, checks the policy, keys the PO, chases the confirmation, matches the invoice. 80–90% of demand still arrives as unstructured free text that software forms were never able to capture — so people route around the software (29% maverick spend, sub-40% engagement with clunky tools). Buying more software deepened the illusion; it never touched execution.

## Vs the legacy *operating models* (not just software)

- **Vs in-house manual ops:** 85% of transactional load automated; humans re-roled to supervision. The manufacturer case: 75% of scope automated in 6 months → 99.6% at 12 months.
- **Vs BPO/SSC:** same scope executed at ~7% of BPO cost, 93% cost reduction, without offshore latency, quality variance, or black-box opacity — and the enterprise owns the execution layer and its data. BPO can't counter: its business model *is* the labor arbitrage Lio deletes.
- **Vs "do nothing" (email/Excel):** the largest segment. Lio gives these organizations a front door + automation without forcing them through a suite implementation first. Note: this also means Lio does not *require* a legacy suite to exist — it can be the first real procurement system a company's employees ever touch.

## Why incumbents can't easily copy this (and where they can)

**Structural advantages Lio holds:**
1. **Cross-system scope.** Joule agents live inside SAP; Navi inside Coupa. Real requests flow across ERP + inbox + contracts + the open web — the seams are exactly where suites can't follow without cannibalizing their own UI value.
2. **Clean-slate agent architecture.** Incumbent agents are bolt-ons to 20-year-old data models; Lio's AOP layer was designed agent-first. Incumbents are ~12–24 months behind on shipped cross-stack capability (SAP's Intake Agent went GA only June 2026).
3. **Business-model freedom.** Suites monetize seats/modules; pricing against labor savings would cannibalize their license base. Lio has no such conflict.
4. **Speed as culture.** 2-week deployments with FDEs vs quarterly suite release cycles and SI ecosystems.

**Where the moat is thin (be honest about this):**
- Suites can bundle "good-enough" agents at zero price (SAP gave Joule Agent Runtime free through 2026; Coupa bought Tonkean). Distribution beats product when the CFO already pays the suite invoice.
- Every claimed metric is company-reported; a skeptical buyer will discount 85%/95%/100% until POC-verified — which is why the $10M Challenge exists (risk reversal).
- Depth specialists (Pactum in negotiation, Globality in sourcing) can beat any single Lio agent on its own turf; Lio's bet is that the *integrated workforce* beats the sum of point solutions.
