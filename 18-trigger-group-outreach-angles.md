## 1 · Vitesco Technologies — warm expansion via Schaeffler (NOT cold outreach)

**Trigger:** Vitesco's back-office processes are being consolidated into Schaeffler Global Services Europe (Wrocław) after the Oct 2024 merger. **Schaeffler is an existing Lio customer with a public COO endorsement** (Andreas Schick, [07](07-customers-and-proof-points.md)).

**Angle — "the integration IS the automation moment":** every Vitesco process being re-mapped into Schaeffler GBS gets touched exactly once. Each one can be re-mapped to an Agent Operating Procedure instead of a Wrocław headcount. Extending the existing Lio deployment's scope to the integration workstream is cheaper than automating after the migration settles.

**Channel:** internal — the Schaeffler account team requests scope extension; do not approach Vitesco contacts cold (risks the existing relationship).

**Brief for the account team (3 bullets):**
- The Vitesco integration means thousands of P2P/finance processes are being re-documented for Wrocław right now — the once-per-decade window where AOP capture costs nothing extra.
- Proposal: a scoping session with the GBS integration lead — pick one Vitesco process family (order confirmations or invoice matching) to go agent-first instead of migrating to headcount.
- Reference the existing deployment's numbers; no new-vendor onboarding, no new security review — fastest possible expansion path.

## 2 · Zurich Insurance — the renewal anchor

**Trigger:** operational procurement + AP outsourced to Genpact since April 2012 (public record); sourcing leg via Procurian→Accenture; captive Kraków BSC for F&A. Trade press has noted the hub model's distance from stakeholders — treat as a hypothesis to probe, not an accusation.

**Contacts:** Jaime Paiva — Global Head of Procurement Excellence (champion profile). Exec lane: Carmen Puga (COO) via Keil at DPW Amsterdam, not via sequence.

**Angle — "the 2012 model was priced on labor arbitrage; 2026 execution is agentic":** don't pitch a rip-out. Pitch a benchmark: cost-per-transaction of one outsourced spend tower (travel or facilities — both in the original 2012 scope) vs. agentic execution at ~7% of typical BPO cost, 24/7, fully auditable, and sitting *closer* to stakeholders, not further away.

**First touch (EN, sender: AE; subject: "Your P2P cost-per-transaction, 14 years on"):**
> Hi Jaime — the outsourcing model Zurich set up in 2012 was state-of-the-art for its time: labor arbitrage, hub delivery. Our view is that the economics have moved — AI agents now execute operational procurement and AP end-to-end at roughly 7% of typical BPO cost, with a full audit trail and no distance between the work and your stakeholders.
> We'd offer a simple benchmark, not a commitment: pick one spend tower, we model your cost-per-transaction against agentic execution, verified with your own numbers. Two weeks, on top of your existing systems.
> Worth 20 minutes at DPW Amsterdam? — [name]. (If this isn't relevant to your role, tell me and I won't follow up.)

**Objections & landmines:** 14-year incumbent relationship = inertia → keep the ask small (benchmark one tower). Regulated insurer → lead governance with ISO 27001, EU hosting, human-on-the-loop, audit trail vs. BPO opacity. Accenture holds the sourcing leg — stay strictly on the operational-execution scope.

**Event hook:** DPW Amsterdam (Sep 2026) meeting + dinner seat — priority allocation.

## 3 · Innomotics — greenfield carve-out

**Trigger:** PE-owned (KPS) carve-out exiting Siemens infrastructure (legally separate since Jul 2023) — it is standing up its own back office now, with no captive SSC documented.

**Contacts:** Maren Richter — Head of Functional Excellence in Procurement; Nico Reiner — CFO (PE owners = CFO-friendly economics).

**Angle — "skip the SSC generation entirely":** every other company its size spent 2005–2020 building captive centers in Poland or signing BPO deals. Innomotics gets to skip that generation: agent-first procurement operations from day one — no center build-out, no BPO contract, live in 2 weeks, pure opex, scales with the business. For a PE holding, that's structural cost avoidance, not a savings program.

**First touch (EN; DE subject variant: "Backoffice neu aufbauen — ohne Shared Service Center"; subject EN: "Building the back office once — and skipping a generation"):**
> Hi Maren — companies rebuilding their back office after a carve-out usually face a three-way choice: hire, build a shared-service center, or outsource. There's a fourth option now: AI agents that execute procurement operations end-to-end (intake → PO → order confirmation → invoice match) inside whatever ERP estate you land on — live in under two weeks, no center, no contract lock-in.
> Given where Innomotics is in its build-out, the timing is unusually good: nothing legacy to unwind. Happy to show what the first automated process family would look like on your systems — 30 minutes.
> — [name] (Not the right moment? Say so and I'll close the loop.)

**Objections:** "we're mid-transformation, no bandwidth" → that's the point: 2 weeks, one process family, no IT project. PE proof-need → 6-month-ROI flagship case + the $10M Challenge risk-reversal.

## 4 · KOSTAL — automate the migration, not after it

**Trigger:** processes actively migrating to the Budapest Global BSC (opened Oct 2022, ramping toward 300+), in parallel with announced German job cuts (press figure ~950 — verify before citing).

**Contact:** Mara Hillermeier — Head of Procurement Excellence Europe (persona bullseye).

**Angle — "every process you haven't moved yet is a process you don't have to move":** lift-and-shift migrations lose process knowledge and lock in headcount at the destination. AOPs capture SOPs as executable procedures *during* the move — so the not-yet-migrated queue can go agent-first instead of headcount-first, and Budapest ramps to supervision roles rather than transaction roles.

**Framing rule (works-council sensitivity is HIGH):** first touch talks about **migration risk, knowledge retention, and quality** — never about further cuts. The cost story can come later, in the room.

**First touch (DE-first; subject DE: "Prozesswissen sichern, bevor es nach Budapest umzieht"; EN body for reference):**
> Hi Mara — when transactional procurement moves into a new GBS, the hardest part isn't the ramp-up, it's the process knowledge that doesn't survive the handover. We work with industrial groups mid-migration on exactly this: existing SOPs are captured as executable agent procedures, so processes arrive in the new center already automated — and the team there supervises instead of re-keying.
> For the process families still in your migration queue, that can mean skipping the manual stage entirely. I'd value 20 minutes at the BME Symposium in Berlin — or we host a small group at our Bots & Buyers evening the same week.
> — [name] (Kein Interesse? Kurze Antwort genügt, dann melde ich mich nicht mehr.)

**Channel:** family-owned buying culture → room-before-email. The Berlin November double-week (SSOW DACH + BME) is the natural stage; a dinner invite beats a sequence.

## 5 · Generali Austria — the coexistence play

**Trigger:** procurement runs through GOSP — the group platform JV that is 95% Generali, **5% Accenture**. The Austrian branch is primarily IT infrastructure.

**Contact:** Stefan Koller — Head of Operations & CPO (one person owns both sides — single-threaded entry).

**Angle — "your platform, agent-operated":** never pitch against GOSP or Accenture (they're a shareholder, not a vendor to displace). Mirror Lio's suite posture: GOSP stays the platform; agents become the execution layer on top of it. Austria is a contained, low-risk pilot country whose results travel to Milan on their own.

**First touch (EN; DE subject variant: "GOSP behalten — die Ausführung automatisieren"):**
> Hi Stefan — a question rather than a pitch: on the operations side, how much of your procurement execution (request intake, PO handling, order confirmations, invoice matching) still runs through people, even with the group platform in place?
> That gap — platform in place, execution still manual — is exactly what our AI agents close. They operate *on top of* the systems you already run, which makes a country organization like Austria an ideal contained pilot: two weeks to live, results measurable on your own transactions before anything is proposed at group level.
> 20 minutes in Vienna or at BME Berlin? — [name] (Opt out anytime with a one-line reply.)

**Landmines:** group governance sits in Milan → sell the local pilot, not a group deal. Accenture will see everything — keep the framing complementary until the pilot has numbers.
